ANXIETY DISORDER
================================================================

DESCRIPTION:
Anxiety disorder is characterised by persistent and excessive worry
that is difficult to control. The person may experience physical
symptoms including rapid heartbeat, sweating, trembling, and
shortness of breath. Anxiety can significantly impact daily
functioning and quality of life.

REACTIVE STRATEGIES:
When a person with anxiety disorder becomes distressed, support
workers should respond in the following ways. Speak in a calm,
quiet, and reassuring voice at all times. Remove the person from
the triggering environment if it is safe and appropriate to do so.
Use grounding techniques such as slow deep breathing exercises to
help the person regulate their emotions. Avoid making demands or
adding additional pressure during periods of distress. Validate
the persons feelings without reinforcing avoidance behaviours.
Maintain a calm physical presence and avoid sudden movements.

PROACTIVE STRATEGIES:
To prevent anxiety-related behaviours of concern, support workers
should maintain consistent and predictable daily routines. Provide
advance notice of any changes to routine or environment. Create a
calm and predictable support environment. Encourage regular
physical activity as part of the daily routine. Support the person
to develop and practice personalised coping strategies. Monitor
for early warning signs of anxiety and intervene early.

TRIGGERS:
Common triggers for people with anxiety disorder include unexpected
changes to routine, crowded or noisy environments, unfamiliar
people or situations, and performance-related situations.

SCHIZOPHRENIA
================================================================

DESCRIPTION:
Schizophrenia is a serious mental health condition characterised
by distortions in thinking, perception, emotions, language, and
behaviour. The person may experience hallucinations, delusions,
and disorganised thinking. Episodes can vary in frequency and
intensity and may be triggered by stress or medication changes.

REACTIVE STRATEGIES:
When supporting a person experiencing a schizophrenia episode,
support workers should remain calm and avoid confrontation at all
times. Use simple, clear, and direct communication. Do not argue
with or challenge delusional beliefs as this can increase distress.
Ensure the environment is safe, quiet, and free from unnecessary
stimulation. Maintain a safe physical distance and respect personal
space. Contact the support coordinator if behaviour escalates or
if there is risk of harm to the person or others.

PROACTIVE STRATEGIES:
To reduce the frequency of behavioural episodes, support workers
should ensure consistent medication management in line with the
prescribed plan. Maintain regular and predictable sleep schedules.
Minimise exposure to stressful situations and environments. Monitor
for early warning signs such as increased agitation or withdrawal.
Maintain regular contact with the mental health treatment team.
Encourage and support participation in meaningful daily activities.

TRIGGERS:
Common triggers for people with schizophrenia include medication
non-compliance, high stress environments, disrupted sleep patterns,
substance use, and significant life changes or losses.

AUTISM SPECTRUM DISORDER
================================================================

DESCRIPTION:
Autism Spectrum Disorder is a neurodevelopmental condition
characterised by differences in social communication, sensory
processing, and behavioural flexibility. People with ASD may
have strong preferences for routine and predictability and may
experience distress when these are disrupted.

REACTIVE STRATEGIES:
When a person with ASD becomes distressed, support workers should
reduce sensory input by dimming lights and minimising noise where
possible. Use calm, simple, and clear language. Allow the person
time and space to regulate without pressure. Offer access to
preferred sensory items or comfort objects. Avoid physical
intervention unless there is immediate risk of harm. Follow
the persons established calming strategies as documented
in their support plan.

PROACTIVE STRATEGIES:
To prevent distress and behaviours of concern, support workers
should maintain highly consistent daily routines. Use visual
schedules and supports to assist with transitions. Provide advance
warning of any changes to routine or environment. Identify and
minimise known sensory triggers. Support the development of
communication skills and alternative ways to express needs.
Ensure the environment is sensory-friendly and predictable.

TRIGGERS:
Common triggers for people with ASD include unexpected changes
to routine, sensory overload, transitions between activities,
communication difficulties, and unfamiliar environments or people.

================================================================
INTELLECTUAL DISABILITY
================================================================

DESCRIPTION:
Intellectual disability is characterised by significant limitations
in intellectual functioning and adaptive behaviour. People with
intellectual disability may have difficulty with communication,
self-care, and independent living skills. Behaviours of concern
often serve a communicative function.

REACTIVE STRATEGIES:
When supporting a person with intellectual disability who is
displaying behaviours of concern, support workers should remain
calm and avoid displaying frustration. Use simple and clear
language matched to the persons communication level. Identify
and respond to the underlying need or function of the behaviour.
Offer alternative ways to meet the persons need. Reduce demands
temporarily until the person is calm. Document the behaviour and
any identified triggers for review.

PROACTIVE STRATEGIES:
To reduce behaviours of concern, support workers should ensure
consistent and predictable environments and routines. Use
communication strategies matched to the persons needs including
visual supports and simplified language. Ensure all basic needs
are met including food, drink, rest, and comfort. Provide
opportunities for choice and control in daily activities.
Build positive relationships based on trust and respect.

TRIGGERS:
Common triggers include unmet basic needs, communication
difficulties, changes to routine, sensory discomfort, and
unfamiliar people or environments.
